Toward a Sustainable Future: Biofiber
(The article is published in Ei Samay News Paper, Times of India Group)
by
Dr. Goutam Kulsi
goutam.kulsi@gmail.com
The stock of fossil fuels is limited, but the risk is limitless. So, eliminating petroleum-based
products from daily life is not only difficult but also not impossible. Keeping in mind the
issue of environmental pollution, people are turning away from petroleum-based products
towards bio-based products. They are searching for 'sustainable' solutions, i.e., renewable
natural resources for regeneration and finding alternative pathways. It will be long-lasting,
environmental friendly as well as providing sustainability to the environment and being
beneficial to the humanity.
The demand for bio-materials made from renewable resources is now global. Scientists and
technologists around the world are engaged creating highly useful products such as bio-
based fuels, fibers, films, foams, and fabrics, whose main components are bio-molecules like
cellulose, starch, or proteins. The goal is to create biodegradable and compostable products
using adequate and sustainable use of natural resources, which will significantly reduce
greenhouse gas emissions.
Bio-fibers obtained from renewable resources mainly are two types: plant and animal fibers.
Plant fibers refer to natural fibers obtained from plants, such as cotton, jute, hemp,
bamboo, flax, sisal, kenaf, pineapple leaves, etc. Plant biomass is first separated into
monomers, and then these monomers are polymerized to create high molecular weight
polymers through a polymerization process. Then, through appropriate spinning processes,
these polymers are transformed into fibers. On the other hand, animal fibers include wool
from sheep, silk from silkworms, etc., which have been used in the textile and fashion
industry for centuries.
The demand for bio-fibers has increased due to awareness of circular economy practices
and reducing carbon footprints. For a health conscious society, the significant demand for
bio-fibers is evident. The popularity of bio-fibers in the textile, packaging, and fashion
industries is rising. Many renowned brands are incorporating bio-fibers into their products.
Bamboo fiber is used to create special types of socks, high-quality bathrobes, towels, and
sanitary napkins with anti-bacterial properties. Hemp or cannabis plant fibers are used to
create high-quality clothing fabrics. Jute fiber is used to make 'Sonali Bags,' which serve as
alternatives to non-biodegradable plastic bags. Companies like Pact, No Nasties, and
Grassroot are bringing fashionable dresses, sanitary napkins, and everyday essentials bags
into the market, utilizing bio-fibers. Various startups are working on new ideas using plant-
based fibers both nationally and internationally.
The European Union and other developed countries have long shifted their focus towards
bio-based fibers, imposing restrictions on petroleum-based synthetic fibers. Despite of
having various bans on the use of petroleum-based products worldwide, countries like India
still witness extensive usage of plastic bags in the market. It is long back that the plastic bags
below 50 microns were prohibited in this country. But the use of it in the markets has been
so rampant that now we are at the point of backtracking. We keep mum, like silent
spectators, let alone raise a protest against this malpractice. Ours are nuclear families in
theory, but we do not at all live upto this ideal. For we use separate bags for separate
vegetables. What an irony! The question also arises how the very coffee cups in which lies
the plastic lining, can be biodegradable. Perhaps in the days ahead we'll cease to exist while
in search of light in the womb of darkness or darkness in the womb of light, and then, to be
true, though hard a reality, we'll have no scope at all to raise the slogan of not being
"sustainable".
